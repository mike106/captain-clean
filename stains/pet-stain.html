Pet Stain Removal

Pet urine isn’t just a stain—it's an odor source. You need to blot, treat, and rinse. Enzyme cleaners work best, but only if they’re allowed to dwell and dry properly.

What you’ll need

  • Paper towels or white towels
  • Enzyme-based pet stain/odor remover
  • Warm water in a spray bottle
  • Optional: wet/dry vac or extractor

Step-by-step

  1. Blot immediately. Stand on towels to pull moisture up (don’t rub).
  2. Apply enzyme cleaner per label directions. Cover the full spot + edges.
  3. Let it dwell. Typically 10–15 minutes (or as directed).
  4. Blot/extract the moisture again.
  5. Light rinse with warm water and blot once more (reduces residue).
  6. Dry completely. Fan helps. Odor often returns if it stays damp.

Old/set-in urine may have soaked into padding. If odor persists after drying, it likely needs professional flush/extraction.

Avoid this

  • Steam cleaning fresh urine (heat can set odor)
  • Over-soaking the area (pushes urine deeper)
  • Using strong bleach/ammonia (unsafe + can attract re-marking)
